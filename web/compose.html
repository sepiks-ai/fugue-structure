Write a Fugue

Describe the fugue you would like: a key, a mood, how many voices, how fast. It is composed here in your browser, on an entry plan taken from one of Bach's fugues in the Well-Tempered Clavier.

How it works: your words set the key, mode, number of voices, speed and the character of the subject. A short theme (the subject) is composed to match. A plan model, learned from the 48 fugues of the Well-Tempered Clavier, decides when each voice states the subject, in which key, whether in stretto and whether upside down. A small neural network trained on the same 48 fugues then writes the music around those entries, semiquaver by semiquaver: each voice hears what the others have just played and what the subject is about to do. The line it writes against the first answer becomes the countersubject and returns later; between groups of entries, fragments of the subject move through sequences. Harmony, rhythm and the treatment of dissonance are learned from Bach, not written as rules. Training data: Kyle Rother's open edition of the fugues (CC BY 4.0). Harpsichord, organ and piano recordings from the Versilian Community Sample Library (CC0). More in the article, on the Fugue Map, and in the code.
